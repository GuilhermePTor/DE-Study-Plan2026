Monthly Active Users
in SQL

A step-by-step walkthrough of the User Retention problem — finding MAUs for July 2022 using self-join and CTE patterns.

02 — Problem Breakdown

What does "Active" mean?

Definition: A user is considered a Monthly Active User (MAU) only if they performed at least one action in both the current month (July 2022) and the previous month (June 2022).

This means you can't just count users in July — you need to verify their presence in June too. That two-set intersection is the whole game.

03 — Strategy

Three-step approach

1
Get the distinct users who acted in July 2022 — our candidate MAU set.
2
Get the distinct users who acted in June 2022 — the lookback set.
3
INNER JOIN the two sets on user_id — only users in both survive. Count them.
04 — Solution

CTE approach (recommended)

solution.sql
-- Step 1: users active in July 2022
WITH july_users AS (
    SELECT DISTINCT user_id
    FROM   user_actions
    WHERE  EXTRACT(MONTH FROM event_date) = 7
      AND  EXTRACT(YEAR  FROM event_date) = 2022
),

-- Step 2: users active in June 2022 (the lookback month)
june_users AS (
    SELECT DISTINCT user_id
    FROM   user_actions
    WHERE  EXTRACT(MONTH FROM event_date) = 6
      AND  EXTRACT(YEAR  FROM event_date) = 2022
)

-- Step 3: intersect and count
SELECT
    7                   AS month,
    COUNT(j.user_id)   AS monthly_active_users
FROM  july_users j
INNER JOIN june_users jn
    ON j.user_id = jn.user_id;
05 — Alternative

Subquery approach (compact)

Equivalent result, fewer lines — useful in environments that don't support CTEs.

alternative.sql
SELECT
    7                       AS month,
    COUNT(DISTINCT user_id) AS monthly_active_users
FROM  user_actions
WHERE EXTRACT(MONTH FROM event_date) = 7
  AND EXTRACT(YEAR  FROM event_date) = 2022
  AND user_id IN (
      SELECT DISTINCT user_id
      FROM   user_actions
      WHERE  EXTRACT(MONTH FROM event_date) = 6
        AND  EXTRACT(YEAR  FROM event_date) = 2022
  );
06 — Explanation

Why each part matters

DISTINCT user_id

One row per user per CTE — prevents a user with 10 actions in a month from counting as 10.

INNER JOIN

Only keeps users present in both months — directly encodes the MAU definition.

COUNT(j.user_id)

After the join, each row is one unique MAU. No extra DISTINCT needed here.

Hardcoded 7 AS month

The question asks for the numerical month in the output. No aggregation needed since we target exactly one month.

EXTRACT(MONTH / YEAR)

Safely slices by month without being tripped up by time components in the datetime column.

Two CTEs

Separating July and June into named sets makes the logic readable and debuggable — inspect each CTE independently.
